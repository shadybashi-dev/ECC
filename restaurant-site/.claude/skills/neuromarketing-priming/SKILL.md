# Neuromarketing & Priming

## 0. Read this first — the honest position on "subliminal"

There is a hard distinction, and getting it wrong wastes money and creates legal
exposure.

**Literal subliminal messaging** — stimuli flashed below the perceptual threshold
(masked images, single-frame inserts, audio below conscious hearing — **does not
reliably change purchase behaviour**. The classic claims come from
methodologically weak studies, and the strongest ones failed replication. It is
also explicitly banned or restricted in advertising in a number of jurisdictions,
and platforms reject it. **Do not build it. It would not work anyway.**

**Priming** — suprathreshold stimuli that are fully visible but shape how the
next stimulus is evaluated — **is real, robust, and completely legal.** A warm
palette genuinely changes how appetising a food photo reads. A gaze cue genuinely
moves attention to a CTA. Sensory language genuinely triggers gustatory
simulation. This is the entire legitimate field, and it is what this skill
implements.

Everything below is priming: visible, honest, and effective. Nothing here
deceives the visitor — the visitor would agree with the impression if asked.

## 1. Color — appetite is a learned association, not magic

Color effects are **contextual**, not universal. "Red makes people hungry" is
folklore; the defensible version is narrower.

### What the evidence supports

- **Warm, high-arousal hues** (reds, oranges, ambers) raise arousal and are
  associated with ripeness, cooked Maillard browning, and heat — all appetite
  cues learned over a lifetime. They work *on food imagery*, not as abstract
  decoration.
- **Saturation matters more than hue.** Dull, desaturated food color reads as
  stale. The single most reliable visual appetite driver is **vivid, accurate
  color in the food itself**.
- **Blue suppresses appetite** in food contexts — very few naturally blue foods
  exist, so blue reads as artificial or spoiled. Use blue for UI chrome,
  information, and trust signals; **never tint the food**.
- **Green primes freshness, health, and provenance** — effective for salads,
  vegan options, farm-to-table positioning.
- **Black/dark backgrounds raise perceived premium-ness and increase contrast**,
  which makes warm food colors pop. Dark surfaces also read as "evening" and
  "late night" — a direct match for a kitchen open until 2 AM.
- **Cream/beige + terracotta + serif** is the most over-used AI-generated
  restaurant palette in 2026. It primes "template", not "food". Avoid it
  specifically because it is a recognisable tell.

### Working palettes for a pizza / Italian + Mediterranean late-night brand

| Palette | Primes | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Charcoal + Ember + Bone** | fire, grill, oven heat, evening | Late-night, dough-forward, wood-fired |
| **Olive + Tomato + Semolina** | Italy, provenance, daylight freshness | Family, lunch, traditional |
| **Midnight + Brass + Chili** | premium evening, neighbourhood gem | Date night, wine, reservations |
| **Plaster + Basil + Copper** | Mediterranean, coastal, clean | Fresh, salad-forward, daytime |

Rules:

- One high-arousal accent color, used **only** for food-related actions
  (order, add to cart). Everything else stays neutral. The accent becomes a
  conditioned signal.
- Never let the palette fight the photography. Sample the dominant hue from the
  actual dish photos, then build around it.
- Check contrast on the real composition — white text over a bright pizza fails.
- Keep the same accent hue across the ordering funnel. Changing it at checkout
  breaks the conditioned association at the worst moment.

## 2. Gaze cueing — where people look is controllable

- **Faces and eyes on the page redirect attention.** If a person in a hero photo
  looks at the CTA, viewers look at the CTA. If they look at the camera, viewers
  look at the face. Choose deliberately.
- **Hands and gesture** work the same way — a hand reaching toward a slice
  pulls the eye along that vector.
- **Pointing lines** (a pizza peel, a table edge, a knife, steam rising) can be
  composed to lead toward the order button.
- **Faces raise trust and dwell time** — a real chef or real staff member
  outperforms stock imagery for a local restaurant, because recognisable local
  people are the actual differentiator versus chains.
- Do **not** place a face looking directly at the visitor next to your most
  important CTA; you will lose the click to the face.

## 3. Sensory language — gustatory simulation

Reading a vivid sensory description produces measurable simulation of the
eating experience. This is the cheapest priming tool available and it compounds
with the +27% descriptive-name effect.

Target the senses in this order of effectiveness for food:

1. **Temperature** — "pulled from the oven", "molten", "still bubbling",
   "ice-cold"
2. **Texture / sound** — "crackling crust", "cheese pull", "twice-fried",
   "shatters", "gives under the knife"
3. **Smell** — the strongest memory-linked sense: "oregano hits you when you
   open the box", "garlic butter", "woodsmoke"
4. **Taste specificity** — "sharp pecorino", "sweet San Marzano", "hot honey"
5. **Sight** — "blistered leopard-spotting on the crust", "yolk breaks and runs"

Formula for a dish line:
**[moment of eating] + [dominant texture or sound] + [one smell or taste
specific] + [what makes it unlike the generic version]**.

> "Blistered 28-inch crust that crackles when you cut it, oregano and garlic
> the second you open the box — the SLO seasoning nobody else uses."

Avoid: "mouth-watering", "delicious", "to die for", "best in town". These are
**processing-fluency dead ends** — the brain discounts them as advertising and
they generate no simulation.

## 5. Ambient and contextual priming

- **Steam, motion, and heat** in imagery or subtle animation prime freshness and
  immediacy. A slow steam wisp on the hero outperforms a static photo.
- **Wood, stone, flour, metal** textures prime craft and handmade. Glossy
  plastic gradients prime fast food and lower price expectation. Match the
  surface to the actual price point.
- **Grain/noise** adds warmth and reduces the sterile "generated" feel — use
  lightly, at 2–4% opacity.
- **Time-of-day context.** If the visitor arrives at 11 PM, the site should look
  like 11 PM: darker, warmer, late-night menu forward, "open until 2 AM"
  prominent. This is legitimate personalisation from publicly available local
  time — not surveillance.
- **Portion and scale cues.** Showing a hand, a fork, or a familiar object next
  to a 28" pizza communicates size far better than the number. Scale ambiguity
  is a real purchase blocker for large-format items.
- **Table setting context.** A pizza on a bare white background reads as frozen
  retail. The same pizza on a worn wooden table with a checked napkin and a
  drink reads as a restaurant. Context is doing the work.

## 5. Motion and sound — arousal control

- Motion raises arousal and directs attention, but **non-user-triggered motion
  in more than one place at a time reads as chaos** and lowers trust.
- One orchestrated moment beats scattered effects. A single hero reveal on load,
  then discipline everywhere else.
- `transform` and `opacity` only. Animating layout properties causes jank, which
  *feels* cheap regardless of how good the design is.
- **Never autoplay sound.** It is the fastest trust-destroyer on the web. If
  you want audio, offer it as an explicit control.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — always. Vestibular disorders are real and
  the fallback must still be beautiful.
- Speed of motion communicates brand tempo: slow eases read as premium and
  relaxed; short snappy eases (120–180 ms) read as efficient and fast-service.
  A late-night slice joint wants snappy; a reservation-led dining room wants slow.

## 6. Typography as a priming device

- **Weight and contrast prime price.** High-contrast serif and light weights
  read premium; heavy geometric sans reads modern and casual; condensed bold
  reads loud, fast, urban.
- **Do not use letter-spacing on Arabic** (breaks ligatures) and use it
  sparingly on Latin display type only.
- **Numeral style matters on prices.** Tabular, medium-weight numerals read as
  trustworthy and precise. Decorative or italic numerals on prices reduce
  confidence.
- One display face + one body face. Three is already too many; the eye reads
  inconsistency as low quality.

## 7. The ethics line — hold it

Legitimate priming makes a good product look like what it actually is.
Deception makes a mediocre product look better than it is.

**Do:**
- Make real food look as appetising as it genuinely is (accurate color, good
  light, real steam)
- Lead attention to information that helps the visitor decide
- Use sensory language that matches the actual dish
- Match atmosphere to real atmosphere

**Do not:**
- Show a dish styled with inedible substitutes (motor oil for syrup, glue for
  milk) — it arrives and disappoints, and the review cost exceeds the
  conversion gain
- Inflate portion appearance beyond what is served
- Manufacture urgency, scarcity, or social presence that is not real
- Use masked/below-threshold stimuli — ineffective *and* restricted
- Target known vulnerability states (compulsive eating, intoxication) with
  pressure tactics

A restaurant is a repeat-purchase business. Every priming technique that creates
an expectation the kitchen cannot meet is borrowing conversion from next month
at a very high interest rate.

---

## 8. Application checklist

Before shipping any food-facing surface:

- [ ] Dominant palette sampled from the actual food photography
- [ ] One high-arousal accent, reserved for food actions only
- [ ] No blue cast anywhere on food imagery
- [ ] Hero face/hand/gaze vector leads toward the primary CTA (verified by screenshot)
- [ ] Every dish description contains temperature, texture, or smell — not adjectives
- [ ] Scale cue present on large-format items
- [ ] Context surface (wood/stone/linen) matches the real price point
- [ ] One orchestrated motion moment; everything else quiet
- [ ] No autoplay audio; `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured
- [ ] Two typefaces maximum; tabular numerals on prices
- [ ] Nothing shown that the kitchen cannot deliver tonight
